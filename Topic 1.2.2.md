# Topic 1.2: Geographic Data (Part 2: Geospatial Data & Sources)

----------

### Types & Formats of Geospatial Data

> **Geospatial Data:** Information tied to specific locations on Earth, including natural landforms, infrastructure, and human activities/demographics.

| Presentation Format | Strengths | Use Cases / Examples |
|-|-|-|
| **Maps** | Visualizes spatial patterns and broad distributions. |Global life expectancy regions. |
| **Graphs** | Displays trends over time across different variables. | Life expectancy changes from 1770 to 2015. |
| **Data Tables** |Provides exact quantitative numbers and precise changes.

Absolute and relative demographic changes by country.

**Infographics**

Combines key stats with visuals for easy public comprehension.

Comparative male vs. female life expectancy statistics.

----------

### Sources of Geospatial Data

#### 1. Individual & Field Sources

-   **Fieldwork & Surveys:** Interviews, population censuses, land surveys, sketches, and personal observations.
-   **Smartphone Locational Data:** Photos with embedded geospatial metadata (EXIF/GPS tags).
-   **App Tracking:** Location-based recommendations generated by software applications.

#### 2. Institutional & Document Sources

-   **Government Policy Documents:** Treaties, international agreements, and legal boundaries.
-   **News & Media:** News articles, videos, and media photojournalism.
-   **Commercial Data:** Corporate collection of smartphone location data for targeted advertising and market research.

----------

### Summary Box

> **Key Takeaway**  
> Geographic data collection relies on both primary fieldwork (field observations, interviews, ground photos) and advanced technology (remote sensing, aerial photography, GPS/smartphone metadata). Geographers synthesize quantitative data (census counts, tables, graphs) and qualitative sources (photographs, news media, interviews) to perform landscape analysis and understand human-environment interactions.
