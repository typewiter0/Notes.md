### Types & Formats of Geospatial Data

> **Geospatial Data:** Information tied to specific locations on Earth, including natural landforms, infrastructure, and human activities/demographics.

### Sources of Geospatial Data

#### 1. Individual & Field Sources

-   **Fieldwork & Surveys:** Interviews, population censuses, land surveys, sketches, and personal observations[cite: 3].
-   **Smartphone Locational Data:** Photos with embedded geospatial metadata (EXIF/GPS tags)[cite: 3].
-   **App Tracking:** Location-based recommendations generated by software applications[cite: 3].

#### 2. Institutional & Document Sources

-   **Government Policy Documents:** Treaties, international agreements, and legal boundaries[cite: 3].
-   **News & Media:** News articles, videos, and media photojournalism[cite: 3].
-   **Commercial Data:** Corporate collection of smartphone location data for targeted advertising and market research[cite: 3].

----------

### Summary Box

> **Key Takeaway**  
> Geographic data collection relies on both primary fieldwork (field observations, interviews, ground photos) and advanced technology (remote sensing, aerial photography, GPS/smartphone metadata)[cite: 3]. Geographers synthesize quantitative data (census counts, tables, graphs) and qualitative sources (photographs, news media, interviews) to perform landscape analysis and understand human-environment interactions[cite: 3].
