# Topic 1.5: Human-Environmental Interaction (Part 1: Concepts & Land Use)

----------

> **Essential Question:** How do human-environmental interaction and major geographic concepts explain spatial relationships and patterns?

----------

### Core Concepts & Natural Resources

> **Human-Environmental Interaction**  
> The dual connection and exchange between humans and the natural world, covering how humans alter physical environments and how environments impact human societies.

> **Natural Resources:** Items occurring in the natural environment that people find useful or beneficial (e.g., air, water, oil, fish, soil, minerals).

|Resource Type|Definition|Key Examples|
|-|-|-|
|**Renewable Resources**|Unlimited resources that will not be depleted by human use.|• Air / Wind power<br>• Surface water & hydroelectricity<br>• Solar energy<br>• Biomass (wood, crops, sewage)|
|**Non-Renewable Resources**|Limited resources that can be exhausted by human consumption.|• Fossil fuels (petroleum, natural gas, coal)<br>• Earth minerals (gold, copper, silver)<br>• Deep aquifer underground fresh water<br>• Soil|

-   **Uneven Access:** Natural resources are distributed unevenly globally. A nation's development level, technology, and financial resources dictate whether it can access and utilize resources within its borders.

----------

### Sustainability & Land Use

-   **Sustainability:** Using current resources in ways that preserve them for future generations while minimizing negative environmental impacts.
-   **Sustainable Policies:** Encouraging a transition toward renewable, lower-emission energy while reducing dependence on non-renewable fossil fuels.
-   **Land Use:** Studying how people utilize, modify, and organize land.
    -   **Natural Environment:** Untouched natural elements such as plants, air, water, and wildlife.
    -   **Built Environment:** Physical human-made artifacts on the landscape (e.g., buildings, roads, signs, farms, fences).
    -   **Cultural Landscape:** The unique built environment created when human cultures interact with their distinct physical surroundings.
