# Topic 1.2: Geographic Data (Part 1: Collection & Landscape Analysis)

----------

> **Essential Question:** What are different methods of geographical data collection?[cite: 3]

----------

### Core Concepts

> **Geospatial Revolution**  
> Modern era of geography defined by advanced data gathering via technical mapping, satellites, aerial imagery, and field observations[cite: 3]. Data quality directly shapes individual choices and policy decisions[cite: 3].

----------

### Landscape Analysis & Observation

> **Landscape Analysis:** The task of defining, describing, and interpreting the conditions and traits of an area of land[cite: 3].

#### 1. Field Observation & Fieldwork

-   **Field Observation:** Physically visiting a location to record firsthand information[cite: 3].
-   **Techniques:** Writing field notes, taking ground-level photographs, sketching maps, measuring, and interviewing residents[cite: 3].
-   **Spatial Data:** Any information that can be tied to specific geographic locations[cite: 3].

----------

#### 2. Technological Advances in Data Gathering

-   **Remote Sensing:** Gathering information using satellites orbiting Earth or high-altitude craft above the atmosphere[cite: 3].
-   **Aerial Photography:** Professional images taken from aircraft within the atmosphere[cite: 3].
-   **Ground-Level Photography:** Replaced manual sketching for capturing visual landscape details[cite: 3].
-   **Environmental Sensors:** Sound recordings and chemical analyses of air, water, and soil[cite: 3].

----------

### Data Interpretation

Once spatial data is gathered, geographers synthesize and integrate diverse sources to analyze changes in rural and urban landscapes over time[cite: 3].

-   **Key Analytical Questions:**
    -   Who is migrating into or out of the area[cite: 3]?
    -   What are the cultural practices of these groups[cite: 3]?
    -   How will migration impact the local economy[cite: 3]?
    -   What drives human movement and human-environment interactions[cite: 3]?
